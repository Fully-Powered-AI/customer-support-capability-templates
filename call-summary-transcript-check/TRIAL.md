## What was observed

What came out: a complete check in the set format, with all nine sections present, the counts adding up (14 = 6 + 2 + 5 + 1; needs the reviewer 14 = 2 + 5 + 1 + 6), and the ombudsman line on the "Raise with" line. Nothing in the instructions told the assistant to stop, and none of the stop conditions applied to these inputs. It did not rewrite the summary, change the record or assess the ombudsman remark.

Compared with the published example, the two agree on every verdict that both contain. Both found the transposed amount ($46.20 against forty-two dollars sixty), the credit recorded as applied when it needed approval, the booking that was only an offer, the dropped condition on the corrected bill, the payment plan the customer refused, "next Wednesday" with an unheard date, the counted period that matches neither reading, and the "resolved" that was not. The differences:

- **Claim count.** At the time of the run, the published example treated "three monthly payments of $104" as one amount claim (13 claims). At the time of the run the template did not say whether a number of payments and a sum in the same phrase are one claim or two, so the assistant made two (S12, S13), giving 14 claims and six supported. Both are defensible.
- **Left out.** The assistant listed one more item (L1, the customer's "around a hundred and forty" for a usual bill, under Sum of money), because the must-carry list says "every sum discussed that was charged, billed" and does not say whether a rough figure counts. At the time of the run, the published example left it out. Both give the end of the month as Monday 30 November 2026; the assistant's first pass left that date reading out and added it on rereading the rule for Left out items.
- **Closing line of S14.** The assistant quotes line 18 as the open step where the published example quotes line 10; either shows a step still to happen.
- **Notes.** The assistant added a note that "eight and twelve" has no am or pm, and a note that the correction says "requested" while the call only shows an offer to request.

Facts not in the inputs: none in the assistant's output beyond the weekdays and dates it worked out from the call date. At the time of the run, the note on S5 in the published example said "the agent requested the credit". The transcript says only "I can request", and the claim that it was requested comes from the later correction. That note goes a little beyond the transcript.

Where the instructions were ambiguous and the assistant had to guess:

1. Whether a number of payments and a sum in one phrase are one amount claim or two (see above).
2. Whether a customer's rough figure for a usual bill is a "sum discussed" for the must-carry list.
3. Whether "Corrected bill to be issued" is a statement or an outcome. It describes something still to come, so the assistant called it a statement, as the published example does.
4. The start point for "within five working days": the template says "once the reading is in", and the assistant counted from the reading date. The day the reading reaches the company could be later. The template also does not say what to do when a reading falls on a closed day (Wednesday 11 November is closed); the assistant counted from it anyway and said nothing more.
5. Whether line 12's "once we have an actual reading we can check it again" is an agent action that needed its own Left out item. The assistant treated it as covered by the corrected-bill claims.
6. Whether two items from one line (L4 and L5, both line 23) are one or two. The assistant made two, since one is a request with an answer and the other a complaint with none.
7. Whether a window given as part of an unconfirmed booking can be "supported" (S8) while the booking itself is not (S6). The assistant supported it for what the agent said.

Shortcomings of the template found in this one trial:

- At the time of the run, the version notes still said "Not yet tried on its invented example". That is no longer true once this record is attached.
- The instruction block says to use the output format "exactly" but does not contain it; the format sits in a separate section the person must paste after the block. This worked here only because both were supplied.
- Nothing says what a recorded closed day means for the day of a booked visit.

This was one run, by one model, on one invented call. It does not show how the template performs on a real transcript, on a long one, or in another product, and the sixteen acceptance tests in the template were not run.

Changed after the run recorded above, and not run again. The output and the observations above are as recorded and describe the template and the example as they stood at the run. The example has since been brought into line with what the run found, so the comparison above can no longer be seen by reading the published example:

- **The example's claim count.** "Three monthly payments of $104" is now two amount claims (S12 and S13), so the example checks 14 claims, six of them supported.
- **The example's Left out.** It now carries L1, the customer's rough figure for a usual bill, and Left out is 6.
- **The example's note on S5.** It now says the agent offered to request the credit and that no line says it was requested, approved or applied.
- **The example's S14.** It now quotes line 10 (the credit awaiting approval), line 23 and line 25 as the open steps; the run quoted line 18 instead.
- **The example's S10 working.** It now says that Wednesday 11 November is a closed day and is counted from all the same.
- **The amount definition.** The template's definition of an Amount claim now names a number of payments, items or units, as well as a sum.
- **The must-carry list.** Item 2 now says that a figure the customer gives roughly for their usual bill counts, and is noted as such.
- **Rule 7.** It now says what to do when the start point of a counted period is a closed day, and that the count runs from the day the event happens, not from its receipt, unless a line says otherwise.
- **Left out in the pasted instructions.** Step 5 now says to read relative dates in a Left out item as in step 4 and to give "Date read as", which the run missed on its first pass.
- **The version notes.** They now say the template was tried once on the example, with this record published, and that later wording changes were not run again.
- **Closed day and a booked visit.** Rule 6 now says that a visit or deadline falling on a closed day under any reading is mentioned in the Why or Note; the example's S7 says so. This answers the shortcoming above, which is recorded as found.
- **The ambiguities in items 5 to 7 above.** New rules 9 and 10 in step 4 cover a detail inside an unsupported claim (the window in S8, the figures in S12 and S13) and a conditional remark with no commitment; step 5 now gives one Left out item per must-carry label per line.
- **Later corrections and supported notes.** A correction can now be supported in part, and the format allows a Note on a supported claim; the example's correction line and the notes on S8, S12 and S13 use these. The pasted instructions and step 3 now say the same of a correction that says the transcript is wrong at a line.
- **Related workflows.** The entry now lists the call promises workflow as related.
